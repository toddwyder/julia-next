# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users
One household with two Google accounts. Todd plans the menus, does almost all the cooking and most of the shopping, and clips recipes. He plans and clips on the desktop (Chrome) and the phone. Tina cooks and shops occasionally, so the screens must work for an occasional user without any learning. Julia is installed to the home screen on phones and tablets. Whether the kitchen tablet is used while cooking is undecided.

## Product Purpose
Julia is a household cookbook that goes beyond storing recipes: it invents new recipes (Lab), plans meals (Menus, Prep list) and handles the shopping (Shopping list, Cart Mode, Voice add). Success means Todd can go from an idea to a cooked meal in one place.

## Positioning
Other recipe apps mainly store recipes. They can't help invent a recipe or plan a menu. Julia does both and ties them to the cooking and shopping that follow.

## Operating Context
- Planning and clipping at the desktop or on the phone; Clipper in Chrome, share-to-Julia on phones.
- Cooking from the recipe page and Cook mode at the stove, often mid-task.
- Shopping in store with Cart Mode on the phone.
- Recipes come from web links, pasted text, cookbooks the household owns, and the Lab.

## Capabilities and Constraints
- Library: one alphabetical list, search across title, ingredients and source, a Recent strip of 5, and an Under 30 minutes switch. No folders, tags or categories.
- Recipe: stored as written. Scaling, conversions (imperial/metric), total time and nutrition are worked out on display.
- Every recipe shows a Score (0–100, "~" when estimated) and Glycemic load (low ≤10, medium to 19, high ≥20), plus the per-serving Nutrition panel, which is always an estimate.
- Shared page: the only public surface, for read and print. Book recipes never appear on one in full.
- Use the glossary's terms (Library, Recipe, Yield, Cook mode, Lab, Menu, Prep list, Shopping list) in the UI and docs.

## Brand Commitments
- The vintage cookbook feel is fixed: a cookbook that has been used and loved, never new or sterile. No food photos.
- Julia is named for Julia Child, who inspired Todd to cook; "established 1961" refers to that.
